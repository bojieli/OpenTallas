# Code of Conduct

## Our standard

OpenTallas is a research repository whose value rests on people being willing to
say that a number is wrong. That only works if disagreement is cheap and safe.

Everyone participating in this project — issues, pull requests, reviews,
discussions — is expected to:

- **Engage with the evidence, not the person.** "This artifact's sources have
  drifted, so its figure is inadmissible" is the right shape of criticism.
  Comments about the competence or motives of whoever produced it are not.
- **Accept correction without defensiveness, and offer it without contempt.**
  This repository's own history includes a post-mortem titled *"how the
  accelerator came to be 44,000× too slow"* and several published claims that
  were later withdrawn. Being wrong in public is part of the method here, not a
  failure of it.
- **Say what you actually verified.** Overstating what you ran, or presenting an
  inference as a measurement, wastes other people's time and damages the
  evidence chain the project depends on. "I did not check this" is always an
  acceptable thing to write.
- **Assume competence and good faith.** Ask what someone meant before
  concluding they were careless.

## Unacceptable behaviour

Harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory language, sustained disruption,
publishing others' private information, and unwelcome sexual attention are not
tolerated. Neither is persistently relitigating a settled technical decision
after it has been decided with reasons given.

This applies equally to maintainers. Holding more of the context is not a
licence to be dismissive.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces — the repository, its issues and pull
requests, and any communication where someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns to the maintainer at the address in the repository's git
history. Reports will be read, taken seriously, and kept as confidential as
addressing them allows.

Because this is a small project, there is no separate ethics body: the
maintainer handles reports directly. If your concern is *about* the maintainer
and you are not comfortable raising it with them, say so publicly in an issue —
a project that cannot tolerate that criticism does not deserve contributions.

## Consequences

Maintainers may edit, hide, or delete contributions that violate this document,
and may block accounts for repeated or serious violations. Where a judgement
call is made, the reasoning will be stated rather than left implicit.
