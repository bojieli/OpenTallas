# V4.1 physical floorplan and placement contract

## Objective and evidence

Minimize complete single-user decode latency first. Then fill otherwise idle stages with independent users without increasing that latency or exceeding the physical budgets. Root owns this contract; block owners implement and return measured violations for architectural revision.

This revision is a **geometric reservation and connectivity plan**, not a macro-packed or routed die. [Reservation drawing](../results/floorplan/v41_reservation_plan.svg) and [machine-readable geometry](../results/floorplan/v41_reservation_plan.json) are generated by `tools/v41_floorplan_contract.py`. Geometry tests reject overlaps and overflow. They do not certify capacity, timing, power or performance.

[Resource inventory](V41_FLOORPLAN_RESOURCE_INVENTORY.md) and [connectivity ledger](V41_FLOORPLAN_CONNECTIVITY.md) identify the source-pinned implementation. The historical analytical assembly is a separate resource profile: its collective/controller/router counts do not match current RTL. Neither profile is silently substituted for the other.

## Placement architecture

Keep weight storage and dot-product arithmetic together. Each ROM bank's output pins face its local selection, capture registers and MACs; distribute activations and return results over explicitly registered channels. Do not collect ROM weight traffic into a central shared compute pool.

The drawing reserves four compute/memory regions separated by transport corridors, HBM service along north/south edges and package/external links along east/west edges. Four regions are physical locality partitions, not four additional tensor-parallel ranks. Rank ownership remains a die-level program property. Region assignment must follow integer tensor/expert ownership; balance the critical dependency chain before aggregate area.

The outer die dimensions come from the historical assembly. Internal edge and channel widths are initial root proposals, not package or routing results. They reserve space rather than squeeze content to fit. Exact macro dimensions, halos, power straps, pin access and track demand must validate or replace them. No region has been declared to fit its payload.

| Local group | Placement rule | Required acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ROM/MAC | Pin-facing banks, capture registers and local MAC; activation spine alongside | Integer image placement; operand cadence; local route and power legality |
| VM and collective DMA | Bank VM for the actual simultaneous read/write pattern; terminate collective flits locally | Conflict-free banks, completion order, real port implementation and bounded response |
| Attention | Packed stage, transpose/rotation, stationary stores and engine adjacent | Complete storage bill; producer and consumer cadence; numeric composition |
| Probability producer | Place local probability buffer next to attention and its VM source | Charge actual scalar preload; a wider local ingress cannot hide it |
| HBM service and index | Local per-PC arbitration, bounded queues and regional dispatch | Shared bandwidth accounting and actual physical endpoint; no huge flattened global port bus |
| Cross-region transport | Register at explicit boundaries with credit-controlled queues | Routed delay, queue bounds and all added cycles in token schedule |

Do not stretch the packed KV interface or collective bank interface across the die as flat buses. Keep them inside their consumer group, and use an explicit transport endpoint for a long crossing. The connectivity ledger records widths and units; absent routed distance limits remain unknown.

## ROM capacity screening

The [executable capacity bound](../results/floorplan/v41_rom_capacity.json) uses actual predictive-library macro geometry. For the rounded-up adopted average ROM allocation, 264 useful bits per macro word requires 10,041 macros and 150.640 mm² before logic, muxes, channels or port replication. Repeating the observed local-bank occupancy requires 203.313 mm². These are capacity screens, not tensor placement or complete fit. They cannot replace the separate N5 analytical density assumption. The densest macro also misses the proposed period, so density alone cannot select the memory.

## Latency budgets before utilization

Build a timestamped dependency schedule from actual execution: ROM access and compute, reduction/rounding, collectives, index scan and selected fetch where required, packed KV refill, QK, normalization/probability preload, PV, output projection and layer handoff. Charge startup, drain, backpressure, pipeline registers and clock crossings. The longest dependency path determines single-user latency; peak bandwidth alone does not.

The current narrow probability producer needs 2,560 issue cycles for full-geometry preload. WINDOW replay can issue one beat per cycle with the optional finite-credit path, but serial refill still dominates its measured complete service. These are placement and producer-design constraints, not reasons to claim higher token rates from a faster isolated engine.

First close layer-zero WINDOW composition. Indexed layers additionally require scan, global ID selection, remote ownership and gather; a layer-zero pass does not close those paths. Preserve expert output-row partitioning and the golden rounding points. Physical shortcuts may not substitute a numerically different K-split.

## Secondary request pipeline

After the single-request schedule closes, admit independent requests into idle stages using separate context IDs, bounded queues and explicit KV ownership. Size concurrency from stage initiation intervals and residency, not a desired utilization percentage. Arbitration must reserve the primary latency budget; count interference in the multi-user schedule. Extra context storage and queues require a new area/power/route budget before adoption.

## Physical closure sequence

1. Bind one deployable engine profile and complete integer tensor placement to actual ROM/SRAM macro views. Keep technology assumptions separate; an analytical density is not a physical macro.
2. Pack local groups including logic, port replication, halos, routing and PDN. Reject overflow explicitly. Match macro origins and cell orientations to rows, tracks and rails.
3. Close local pin access, placement, power connectivity and timing. Existing microblock power-grid failures are still open until corrected runs pass; detailed-route DRC alone is insufficient.
4. Assign regional channels from edge traffic and routing capacity; measure representative worst crossings. Update the single-user schedule with their actual latency.
5. Place the full chosen profile with package-compatible PHY abstracts, power grid and clock distribution. Check congestion, setup/hold, electrical limits, IR/EM and thermal density.
6. Re-run exact connected execution and calibrate ROM/HBM comparison using the same schedule and physical constraints. Publish achieved rates only at the verified scope.

Until these gates pass, this plan establishes placement intent and rejection criteria, not an achievable frequency or a complete-token rate.
