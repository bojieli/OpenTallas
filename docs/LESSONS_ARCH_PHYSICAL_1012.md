# Design-methodology lessons (2026-10-12) — for the final write-up

**Context.** Three targets (Qwen ROM, DeepSeek ROM S81, generic HBM accelerator) were built largely bottom-up: functional ISA/RTL first, element-by-element P&R closure next, die-level physical work late (and paused). In October 2026 the HBM die hit 1.37 M global-route overflow (77 % at one central window) and several of its largest elements failed to close after many iterations; its key engines were also 2–33× off their throughput budget.

**Root cause.** A central vector memory (VM) fed 32 SMs, attention, HC and the command processor through two ~17.8 kb lane groups; ≈95 kb of ports met at the spine — 1.70× the usable M6–M9 tracks across the worst cut (results/arch/hbm_die_phy_study_1012/STUDY.md). Weights were already quadrant-local; the hub carried activations. The ROM dies did not have this problem in severe form because their dominant data (weights) sits inside each tile next to compute.

**What an experienced accelerator team would have done.** (1) Physically-aware architecture: cut/bisection bandwidth vs wire capacity per mm, floorplan and clock plan before RTL; (2) continuous die prototyping with placeholder blocks; (3) proven fabrics/tile templates; (4) per-unit throughput qualification at production shape before hardening. The 1.70× cut would have been found in an afternoon.

**What we did.** Re-partitioned the fabric, not the engines: B2 = four clusters, one per HBM stack, each with its own VM slice, SMs, attention and vector units; narrow streamed, credit-based slice ring with no global barriers; MoE experts split across clusters; bit-exact summation order. Cut 1.70 → 0.55; DeepSeek tok/s −0.5 to −2 %; ~85 % of placed area reused. The 2-PHY/2× dies alternative was rejected (cut unchanged, −29 to −55 % tok/s). Qwen ROM moved TP4 → TP8 for the analogous density reason (reticle-full die, 72 % block area).

**Other process lessons.** A top-down hierarchy contract with registered seams and layer rules; a reference tile flow with a pre-route gate; a child-lint gate (face pins blocked above, metal on the strap layer) — 27 of 43 used tiles had "closed" yet were unassemblable because of flow bugs (keepout overridden, hold ECO re-routing after keepout removal); clock spines / ≤15k sinks per root to avoid non-converging hold repair on flat clock trees; ASAP7's 9 metal layers (vs ~12–13+ on production nodes) make the study conservative on routing resources.

**Why it is worth reporting.** It shows the architecture-physical co-design loop is the binding constraint for decode accelerators at reticle scale, quantifies the cost of fixing it late (fabric respin, engines reused), and explains die-count choices (Qwen TP8, HBM 96 dies) as consequences of wiring capacity, not just memory capacity.
